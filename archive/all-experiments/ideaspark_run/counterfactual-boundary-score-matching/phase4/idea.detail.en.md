# Boundary Score Matching for Functional-Geometry Counterfactuals

**Method.** Boundary-Local Score Matching (BLSM)

## Motivation
**Problem framing.** Functional geometry changes define a structural problem class in which the instruction, manipulated objects, and much of a candidate action remain stable while a small change in clearance, pose, or contact geometry changes the feasible action set. A continuous-action VLA trained by ordinary imitation receives no direct signal about that decision boundary. Global feasibility supervision can reject an invalid rollout, but it spreads credit over an entire action chunk and can depress nearby actions that remain physically valid. The core bottleneck is therefore not merely recognizing geometry; it is assigning a feasibility change to the first trajectory segment that actually violates the edited scene while retaining the other valid modes.

The closest adjacent systems address pieces of this structure but leave the credit-and-retention question unresolved. Explicit feasibility supervision targets invalid actions [semanticscholar:dd324415c99589f1f7f9b63eaa3a64a7ae042df2], Action Manifold Learning constrains prediction to geometry-guided valid regions [semanticscholar:8cb14cd9dafae88163a7685336a02ac6c41ed646], KeyStone selects a dominant consistent action cluster [semanticscholar:4a71dc5d2b6b4ea2a6477ec4515d8294e3c75383], GEAR-VLA aligns geometry-aware representations [dblp:journals/corr/abs-2606-08530], and STARRY couples future spatial-temporal latents to actions [semanticscholar:f134eaaeb2cf6d4ac3526993199033b7f858a35b]. None of the available records establishes a matched minimal edit, first-violation credit mask, complement-drift constraint, and mode-wise retention target in one mechanism. BLSM turns that residue into an identifiable training object: the score change that should occur at a certified feasibility boundary, separated from the score changes that should not occur elsewhere.

**Why now.** The gap is timely because recent VLA work has made geometry and continuous-action structure explicit enough to support controlled boundary construction: Pointing-VLA exposes typed spatial grounding [semanticscholar:aaef4e8a3bd4a3a4f6673b2d4bce0a3237783cb9], WNM-3D maintains persistent 3D context [semanticscholar:74257100ad8c273b90dbaaba190e1f699ec9df0a], Action Manifold Learning supplies a geometry-guided validity view [semanticscholar:8cb14cd9dafae88163a7685336a02ac6c41ed646], and KeyStone makes action-mode structure measurable [semanticscholar:4a71dc5d2b6b4ea2a6477ec4515d8294e3c75383]. Together with scripted RoboTwin 2.0 scene edits and a frozen continuous-action reference policy, these ingredients permit paired counterfactuals, simulator-certified violation masks, and per-mode score accounting that earlier aggregate feasibility objectives could not expose.

**Why prior work stopped.**
- `semanticscholar:dd324415c99589f1f7f9b63eaa3a64a7ae042df2` (arXiv 2026-04): Can Explicit Physical Feasibility Benefit VLA Learning? studies a geometry-grounded feasibility objective for suppressing physically invalid robot actions.
  - _Did not do_: Its available abstract does not establish same-action minimum geometry flips, first-violation-local score credit, complement anchoring, or independent retention of every feasible alternative mode.
  - _Structural reason_: The missing ingredient is a paired boundary-construction and temporal-localization instrument; full-text absence also prevents verifying whether any equivalent mechanism is present.
- `semanticscholar:8cb14cd9dafae88163a7685336a02ac6c41ed646` (arXiv 2026-05): Learning Action Manifold with Multi-view Latent Priors predicts actions on a geometry-guided valid manifold to reduce monocular ambiguity and inefficient regression.
  - _Did not do_: It does not use a minimal geometry intervention to measure which formerly valid mode becomes invalid or explicitly preserve worst-case retention across the remaining valid modes.
  - _Structural reason_: Its representation-level manifold objective lacks the paired score-change estimand and mode-indexed preservation mechanism required for boundary-local credit.
- `semanticscholar:4a71dc5d2b6b4ea2a6477ec4515d8294e3c75383` (arXiv 2026-05): Geometry Guided Self-Consistency for Physical AI samples candidate actions and executes the medoid of the largest geometry-consistent cluster.
  - _Did not do_: It does not treat minority feasible clusters as protected positives or test whether feasibility shaping preserves each alternative mode after a geometry flip.
  - _Structural reason_: Largest-cluster aggregation removes mode identity from the objective, so it has no measurement or loss term for compiler-relative worst-mode retention.
- `dblp:journals/corr/abs-2606-08530` (arXiv 2026): GEAR-VLA aligns 3D features and learns a canonicalized continuous-action expert for more generalizable manipulation.
  - _Did not do_: It does not construct identical-action feasibility flips, localize the first violating segment, or separate necessary local score change from unwanted complement drift.
  - _Structural reason_: Its primary object is geometry-aware representation transfer rather than an interventional temporal credit signal with matched retention diagnostics.
- `semanticscholar:f134eaaeb2cf6d4ac3526993199033b7f858a35b` (arXiv 2026-04): STARRY jointly denoises future spatial-temporal latents and actions through depth-conditioned attention for manipulation.
  - _Did not do_: It does not estimate a reference-adjusted margin from a controlled validity flip or constrain action-score drift outside the simulator-certified violation segment.
  - _Structural reason_: Joint future-and-action denoising supplies no matched counterfactual boundary label or unique decomposition-derived supervisory signal.

**What changes when the gap closes.** Closing this gap would let a VLA learn that a particular contact or clearance segment must change after a geometry edit while retaining all other feasible ways to finish the task. It would also make the reliability-diversity trade-off measurable: feasibility methods such as [semanticscholar:dd324415c99589f1f7f9b63eaa3a64a7ae042df2] and geometry-guided action methods such as [semanticscholar:8cb14cd9dafae88163a7685336a02ac6c41ed646] could be judged not only by invalid-action suppression, but by complement drift, alternative-positive coverage, and worst-mode success.

## Method
**Pipeline.** BLSM starts from successful RoboTwin action chunks and compiles the smallest scene-geometry edits that make the identical chunk invalid while preserving the task. Simulator replay identifies the first contiguous violation segment and enumerates the feasible alternative-action roster in the edited scene. A frozen reference policy converts candidate scores into score changes; training then prefers each feasible alternative over the invalid chunk only inside the violation mask, anchors the mask complement, and separately anchors every feasible mode. The resulting post-trained policy is characterized by its held-out boundary margin, edited-scene validity, complement drift, alternative-positive coverage, and worst-mode retention.

### Background
*Construct accepted geometry-flip pairs, certify their first violation segment, and enumerate supported feasible modes.*

1. **Compile minimal feasibility flips** (`S1`)
   - Materialize each source example as a pair record containing $task_{id},$ instruction, simulator snapshot x, the frozen-policy action tensor $a^{-}_{1}:T$ with its control rate, the original replay trace, and compiler provenance. Verify the chunk in x, enumerate the declared task-preserving geometry edits, replay the unchanged tensor from the same initial state in each edited snapshot $x\prime ,$ and retain the edit with minimum C using deterministic tie handling. 【Author decision: predeclare the editable scene parameters and domains, task-invariance constraints, normalization of heterogeneous edits in C, and equal-C tie rule.】 Evaluate original and edited replays with one versioned chunk-level predicate F. 【Author decision: define F per task family through explicit success events, physical-invalidity events, tolerances, duration requirements, and Boolean aggregation.】 Emit a BoundaryFlip record with both snapshots, $\Delta g,$ C, action tensor, traces, simulator and compiler versions, and acceptance status; also emit one ledger row per attempted edit with predicate outcomes and a machine-readable inclusion or exclusion reason.

*The compiler chooses the lowest-cost geometry edit that flips the identical action chunk from feasible to infeasible.*
$$ \Delta g^*=\arg\min_{\Delta g} C(\Delta g)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad F(x,a^-)=1,\;F(x\oplus\Delta g,a^-)=0 \tag{1} $$

   - _Why:_ Holding task and action identity fixed makes geometry-induced feasibility the only intended difference and exposes whether eligible boundary pairs occupy more than a selected corner of the data.
2. **Certify the first violation run** (`S2`)
   - Restore the accepted pair's identical initial simulator state and random state, then replay $a^{-}_{1}:T$ in x and $x\prime $ at the same control rate while logging aligned state, contact, constraint, and task-event fields after every action. Map each edited-scene prefix trace to the binary indicator $v_{t}.$ 【Author decision: define the task-specific monitor set, contact and state tolerances, persistence and clearance rules, and aggregation that produces $v_{t}.$】 Set $\tau $ to the earliest index with $v_{t}$ equal to one, extend through consecutive violating indices until the first cleared index or T, and set $m_{t}$ only on that closed run; its complement marks every other index. If no violating index exists, return the pair to the eligibility ledger with a mask-unresolved reason rather than emitting an empty mask. Output the length-T bit vectors, $\tau ,$ run endpoint, raw supporting events, and synchronized trace identifiers.

*The mask is the first contiguous simulator-certified violation run beginning at the earliest violating time $\tau .$*
$$ \tau=\min\{t:v_t(x\oplus\Delta g^*,a^-_{1:t})=1\},\qquad m_t=\mathbf{1}\!\left[t\in\mathcal{R}(\tau)\right] \tag{2} $$

   - _Why:_ The decomposition is derived from observed simulator structure and yields the unique temporal region on which the geometry edit first changes functional validity.
3. **Enumerate feasible alternative modes** (`S3`)
   - Run the task compiler from the edited snapshot's initial state, with the same instruction, horizon, control rate, and action parameterization as the invalid chunk. Enumerate the compiler's declared solution domain, remove exact duplicate action tensors, replay every candidate in the edited simulator, and retain only candidates accepted by the same chunk-level predicate F. 【Author decision: specify the candidate parameterization, solver or generator, declared solution domain, duplicate rule, and exhaustion or coverage certificate that makes enumeration complete.】 Partition retained chunks with a deterministic mode equivalence relation. 【Author decision: define mode identity through an explicit object and equivalence rule, including any trajectory, contact-sequence, object-side, homotopy, or threshold criteria.】 Select exactly one canonical scored chunk from each supported mode. 【Author decision: define the within-mode representative rule and comparison measure, such as a compiler-designated canonical solution or a declared medoid.】 Emit $A^{+}$ as an ordered roster of records containing $mode_{id},$ the T-by-D action tensor, compiler provenance, replay outcome, representative evidence, within-mode multiplicity, and total roster size K; a mode is supported when at least one enumerated member passes F.
   - _Why:_ A mode-indexed roster makes positive coverage and worst-mode retention observable and prevents dominant-mode aggregation from hiding feasible alternatives.

### BLSM
*Apply boundary-local preference while anchoring the complement and every feasible alternative.*

4. **Match boundary-local score changes** (`S4`)
   - Freeze the pre-training reference checkpoint in evaluation mode and initialize the trainable copy from it. For $x\prime ,$ evaluate one scalar $s_\theta $ and $s_{ref}$ at every action time for $a^{-}$ and each roster representative, using identical observation preprocessing and conditioning context in both policies. 【Author decision: name the continuous-action policy parameterization and define the scalar score, including conditioning context, action-dimension reduction, normalization, and reference computation.】 Before scoring, make every alternative index correspond to the same physical time represented by $m_{t}.$ 【Author decision: require a common T and control rate or define a deterministic alignment map, including unmatched times and phase-shifted contacts.】 Subtract each frozen-reference score from its trainable counterpart to obtain $\Delta _\theta .$ For every mode k, average inside m the difference between the feasible alternative's $\Delta _\theta $ and the invalid chunk's $\Delta _\theta $ to obtain $d_\theta ^(k)$; apply the linked logistic soft preference and average modes equally to produce $L_{B}.$ Emit $L_{B},$ every $mode_{id}$ and $d_\theta ^(k),$ mask cardinality, and the underlying per-time score records.

*Every action score is expressed as a change from the frozen reference policy, preventing raw score scale from defining the preference.*
$$ \Delta_\theta(a_t\mid x')=s_\theta(a_t\mid x')-s_{\mathrm{ref}}(a_t\mid x') \tag{3} $$


*The boundary loss increases the masked reference-adjusted margin of each feasible alternative over the invalid action; $M_{B}$ is the held-out mean margin.*
$$ d_\theta^{(k)}=\frac{\sum_t m_t[\Delta_\theta(a^+_{k,t}\mid x')-\Delta_\theta(a^-_t\mid x')]}{\sum_t m_t},\quad \mathcal{L}_B=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(1+e^{-d_\theta^{(k)}}),\quad M_B=\mathbb{E}[d_\theta] \tag{4} $$

   - _Why:_ Reference adjustment isolates post-training movement, while mask-local preference directs feasibility credit to the segment implicated by the counterfactual rather than the whole action chunk.
5. **Anchor the complement and every positive** (`S5`)
   - For the invalid chunk and all K roster representatives, select indices where $m_{t}$ is zero, square each per-time $\Delta _\theta ,$ average within each chunk, and then average the K+1 chunk values equally to obtain $L_{comp}.$ If m covers all T indices, the linked complement expression has a zero denominator. 【Author decision: choose and record whether full-mask pairs are excluded, contribute no complement term, or receive another explicitly defined treatment.】 Independently, for each feasible mode average squared $\Delta _\theta $ over all T indices and then average the K mode values equally to obtain $L_{alt}$; never weight a mode by compiler frequency or within-mode multiplicity. Output both scalar losses plus per-pair, per-mode, and per-time sufficient statistics so their reductions can be reproduced.

*Complement anchoring limits off-boundary drift, while the equally weighted alternative loss anchors every feasible mode rather than only the dominant one.*
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}=\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{z\in\{a^-,a_1^+,\ldots,a_K^+\}}\frac{\sum_t(1-m_t)\Delta_\theta(z_t\mid x')^2}{\sum_t(1-m_t)},\quad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{alt}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\Delta_\theta(a^+_{k,t}\mid x')^2 \tag{5} $$

   - _Why:_ The complement term prevents temporal credit leakage, and the alternative term makes suppression of minority feasible modes costly even when average validity improves.

### M3_validation
*Select frozen loss weights and expose boundary, validity, drift, coverage, and worst-mode diagnostics.*

6. **Optimize and expose the boundary diagnostics** (`S6`)
   - Construct training batches with ordinary demonstrations and accepted BoundaryFlip records, and apply the base policy's imitation loss to its standard demonstration fields. 【Author decision: name the reference checkpoint and trainable policy class, action representation, conditioning inputs, initialization relation, native imitation loss, and batch reduction.】 Add $L_{B}, L_{comp},$ and $L_{alt}$ with nonnegative $\lambda _{B}, \lambda _{C},$ and $\lambda _{A}.$ On a pair-disjoint validation split, evaluate a predeclared Cartesian set of weight triples, retain only settings that satisfy complement-drift and worst-mode non-degradation constraints, choose the retained setting with greatest boundary separation, resolve ties deterministically, and freeze it before final evaluation. 【Author decision: predeclare the three candidate sets, both non-inferiority margins, the validation statistic and uncertainty rule, and the tie breaker.】 Report $M_{B}$ as the held-out mean of within-pair, equally weighted per-mode margins; report eligibility by attempted and accepted counts plus reason; mask length as count and fraction of T; roster size as K; and complement drift from the held-out complement statistic. Estimate positive coverage and worst-mode retention from policy executions in the edited scenes. 【Author decision: define the rollout distribution, mapping from executions to compiler modes, successful-mode criterion, absolute or reference-relative retention target, and pair/mode aggregation.】 Emit the frozen training configuration, split manifest, selected weights, model checkpoint identifier, and all pair-level records underlying the aggregate diagnostics.

*BLSM adds boundary preference, complement stability, and mode preservation to the ordinary imitation objective; the three weights are selected once on validation pairs and then frozen.*
$$ \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{imit}}+\lambda_B\mathcal{L}_B+\lambda_C\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}+\lambda_A\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{alt}} \tag{6} $$

   - _Why:_ A constrained selection rule prevents an arbitrary loss-weight trade-off from masquerading as the mechanism, while the exposed diagnostics connect score movement to downstream reliability and retention.
